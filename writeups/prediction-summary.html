Predictive modeling of claims status

Published

Abstract

Provide a 3-5 sentence summary of your work on the primary task. Indicate what input data was used, what method was used for binary class predictions, what method was used for multiclass predictions, and what estimated accuracies were achieved.

We classified webpages using text extracted from HTML headings and paragraphs. After preprocessing, word-level text was represented by term frequency–inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) features. A two-hidden-layer neural network achieved 83.6% accuracy on the held-out binary classification test set; a separate five-class neural network achieved 77.9% held-out accuracy. Their five-fold cross-validation accuracies were 80.2% and 81.3%, respectively.

Preprocessing

In one paragraph lay out your preprocessing pipeline. No need to provide exact step-by-step detail; just give an overview of the main components:

Using rvest, we extracted only HTML headings (<h1>–<h6>) and paragraphs (<p>) and combined their text. The parse_fn cleaner removed URLs, emails, apostrophes, punctuation, numbers, symbols, and nbsp artifacts; separated joined lowercase/uppercase words; converted text to lowercase; and normalized whitespace. The reference nlp_fn also specifies stop-word removal, lemmatization, and exclusion of words with two or fewer characters. However, the final model scripts instead call a separate tokenize_fn, so these three additional filters cannot be confirmed for their features. Both models used word tokens (unigrams) and training-derived TF-IDF vectors rather than PCA: up to 7,500 terms for binary classification and 10,000 for multiclass classification, appearing in at least two and in fewer than 90% of training documents. Training vocabularies and IDF weights were applied unchanged to the test data, preserving even webpages with no selected terms.

Methods

Binary classification. We trained a feedforward neural network with two hidden layers of 20 ReLU neurons each, L2 regularization (0.001), and a single sigmoid output for relevant versus nonrelevant claim content. Training used the Adam optimizer and binary cross-entropy loss for up to 20 epochs. An 80/20 stratified train/test split and five-fold cross-validation on the training portion supported model selection; the held-out evaluation used a 20% internal validation split and early stopping (patience 10). Direct TF-IDF inputs performed better than the PCA-based alternatives examined.

Multiclass classification. A separate neural network predicted one of five claim-content categories using two hidden layers of 30 ReLU neurons each, L2 regularization (0.001), and a softmax output. It was trained for 20 epochs with Adam and sparse categorical cross-entropy. To address class imbalance, training observations were weighted using normalized inverse class proportions raised to the power 0.75. We used a stratified 80/20 split and five-fold cross-validation for evaluation. The final full-data model increased the minimum document frequency to three and used a batch size of 32.
